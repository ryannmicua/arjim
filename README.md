# Arjim: My Digital Assistant

Arjim is my digital assistant — the system I talk to about all my work. It isn't a place I go to or an app I open; it's the assistant that runs my projects, keeps me informed, and makes sure nothing slips. This document is a plain-language statement of what Arjim is to me and what I expect from it.

Date: 2026-07-31
Status: Plain-language description of what Arjim is and the outcomes I want from it, derived from a critical review of `docs/models/arjim-scope-model.md`.

## 1. Arjim runs everything for me

Arjim handles any project — start it, check it, move it, close it — without me opening a different app for each one. When a decision needs to be official, Arjim files it in the right place (Planner, SharePoint, email) automatically.

Why I want it: Today I mentally track which tool holds which project, and management happens across half a dozen places. That's overhead on every project.

What changes for me: I have one digital assistant running all my work. The approvals still live where they should officially — I just don't have to handle them myself.

## 2. Arjim brings things to me instead of me chasing them

When something across any project needs my decision, approval, or help, Arjim tells me about it — consolidated into one "this needs me" list. Projects with nothing pending are reported as "nothing," so silence is trustworthy.

Why I want it: Right now, the only way I know something needs me is to visit the project or get pinged in the tool. Things slip when I'm busy.

What changes for me: I stop doing the rounds. I get one trustworthy list, refreshed from the real sources (as of the last check — not real-time, but close).

## 3. Arjim never holds my memory hostage

The facts of my projects — decisions, approvals, scope — live in my project workspaces, not inside Arjim. Arjim keeps only a working copy. If Arjim is wiped or replaced, my project memory survives.

Why I want it: A digital assistant's workspace feels fragile — it's a local folder an agent runs in. My work should outlive any single tool.

What changes for me: I can rebuild, reset, or replace Arjim without fear. The one honest caveat: any project whose only home is Arjim must be moved out first, or I accept that it doesn't get this protection.

## 4. Arjim keeps me posted

Arjim tells me how things are going without me having to ask. Management-level status — where the work is, what's pending, what changed since I last looked — comes to me from Arjim.

Why I want it: Today, "how's it going?" means opening each project. I don't even remember what's open half the time.

What changes for me: I see everything at a glance. One honest limit: Arjim tracks the management of my projects, not their execution — build status, task-by-task progress, and code still need Arjim to go look at the delivery workspace when I ask.

## 5. Arjim is with me on any device

My phone, laptop, work PC — Arjim answers "what am I working on, what needs me, where are the files" with the same answers, wherever I am.

Why I want it: My work moves between devices, and right now paths and setup only work on one machine.

What changes for me: Device stops being a factor in whether I can run my work. This is the furthest-out outcome — the cross-device mechanism isn't designed yet — so I treat it as the goal, not today's reality.

## 6. Arjim works alongside other people's digital assistants

Joven has his own digital assistant on the same projects. When his assistant makes a change, I see it — and I can tell it was his. Arjim picks up the result the next time it reads the shared workspace. Our caches stay separate; only the real records are shared.

Why I want it: I work with other people. Their actions should be visible in the workstream without me running their system or them running mine.

What changes for me: Shared projects behave like shared projects — we both act on the same real records, each through our own digital assistant, and neither of us maintains a shared cache that can drift.

---

## In one line

Arjim runs all my projects, tells me what needs me instead of me hunting for it, never holds my memory hostage, keeps me posted on everything, travels with me across devices, and works cleanly alongside other people's digital assistants — with "management awareness" (yes, automatic) honestly separated from "execution detail" (yes, on request).
